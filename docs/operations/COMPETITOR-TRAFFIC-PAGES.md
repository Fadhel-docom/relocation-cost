# Competitor pages with strongest public demand signals — Relocation Cost

**Research date:** 2026-10-28 UTC  
**Purpose:** identify competitor pages worth studying because they show public evidence of use or strong search-intent fit, then extract useful product/SEO lessons for Relocation Cost.

## Important evidence limit

Competitors' Google Search Console clicks, impressions, CTR and rankings are private. We do **not** have their Search Console accounts. Therefore this is **not** a verified ranking by competitor organic clicks. The evidence below is separated into:
- **Direct public usage claim:** the competitor publishes a usage figure (self-reported; not independently audited here).
- **Search-intent / SERP evidence:** the page appears in searches for relevant calculator queries and visibly serves that intent.
- **No verified page-click figure:** where none is public, no number is invented.

A tool's annual users are not the same thing as organic clicks to one URL. Search results vary by location, device, personalization and date. This report records opportunities, not proof that a page gets a specific number of Google clicks.

## Highest-priority pages to learn from

### 1) moveBuddha — Moving Cost Calculator
- **URL:** https://www.movebuddha.com/moving-cost-calculator/
- **Evidence:** the page explicitly claims **400,000+ movers use its moving cost calculator annually**. This is the strongest public usage signal found in this pass, but it is the site's own claim, not a verified count of organic clicks.
- **Why the page likely satisfies search intent:** one place to estimate cost, compare service types (full-service, containers, rental trucks and labor), and understand assumptions before requesting quotes. It says no email or personal information is required to use the calculator. The site says pricing data is refreshed monthly and describes its data inputs.
- **Useful lesson:** lead with the tool; provide a useful answer before asking for contact details; explain why the estimate changes; make methodology and alternatives visible.
- **Relocation Cost action:** keep the calculator directly accessible from the homepage; make its assumptions and estimate range easy to understand; strengthen the methodology link and explain the distinction between planning estimates and binding quotes. Do not claim equal accuracy without comparable data.
- **Evidence strength:** **High for the competitor's self-reported annual calculator use; no public organic-click count.**
- **Source checked:** 2026-10-28; public page and its visible usage claim.

### 2) Move.org — Moving Costs / 2026 Moving Cost Calculator
- **URL:** https://www.move.org/moving-costs/
- **Evidence:** visible as a direct answer for moving-cost-calculator intent in this research pass; page updated/published Sep 22, 2026 according to the page.
- **What makes it useful:** immediate calculator CTA, cost examples grouped by distance and bedroom count, explanation of local versus long-distance pricing, explicit cost drivers, a comparison of moving methods, and FAQ answers that handle follow-up questions.
- **Useful lesson:** one high-intent page can combine tool + short evidence-based cost examples + concise FAQs, provided each section helps a real decision and is kept current.
- **Relocation Cost action:** review whether our existing calculator and moving-cost guide answer the same follow-up questions without duplicating one another. Where evidence is strong, add a compact “what changes this estimate?” explanation and relevant internal links; do not copy its cost figures.
- **Evidence strength:** **Medium for search visibility and content fit; page-specific clicks are not public.**
- **Source checked:** 2026-10-28.

### 3) Moving.com — Moving Cost Calculator
- **URL:** https://www.moving.com/movers/moving-cost-calculator.asp
- **Evidence:** appeared in this research pass for direct moving-cost-calculator intent. Its public page also displays “recent estimates” and examples grouped by home size, distance, packing service and route.
- **What makes it useful:** demonstrates concrete examples and makes the relationship between origin/destination, home size, packing, move date and estimate explicit. It also connects planning to optional mover quotes.
- **Useful lesson:** examples make an abstract calculator easier to understand, but they must be clearly labeled and traceable to a source or real input.
- **Relocation Cost action:** if we show example scenarios, label the inputs and model version/date, and distinguish examples generated by our model from real customer quotes. Never fabricate “recent user calculations.”
- **Evidence strength:** **Medium for intent match and visible page design; page-specific clicks are not public.**
- **Source checked:** 2026-10-28.

### 4) Forbes Home — How Much Do Movers Cost?
- **URL:** https://www.forbes.com/home-improvement/moving-services/movers-and-packers-cost/
- **Evidence:** appeared in this research pass for moving-cost research. The accessible version of the page gives ranges by distance and bedroom count, hourly labor information, company quote examples, and explains the factors that make prices vary.
- **What makes it useful:** strong research intent coverage for visitors who are not yet ready to use a calculator; tables answer common cost questions directly.
- **Useful lesson:** useful supporting content can bring in visitors earlier in the decision journey and then direct them to the right tool.
- **Relocation Cost action:** strengthen existing explanatory content only where it answers a distinct question (for example, how to interpret a range or which costs are excluded). Cite dated sources for every numerical claim; don't create a thin clone of a competitor's table.
- **Evidence strength:** **Medium for query relevance and content depth; page-specific clicks are not public.**
- **Source checked:** 2026-10-28.

### 5) Bankrate — Cost of Living Calculator
- **URL:** https://www.bankrate.com/personal-finance/cost-of-living-calculator/
- **Evidence:** appeared for cost-of-living calculator research. The page compares metro areas and explains how housing, food, taxes and other costs affect the budget.
- **Why it matters to relocation:** some users are not only asking “what will the move cost?” but also “can I afford to live at the destination?” This is an adjacent intent, not a substitute for a moving-cost calculator.
- **Useful lesson:** a related utility can expand the user's decision without turning one page into an unfocused content hub.
- **Relocation Cost action:** keep this as a **later-stage** product opportunity: consider a destination cost-of-living comparison only after the core moving estimate earns meaningful impressions/clicks and we can source reliable city-level data. Do not add unsupported city cost figures now.
- **Evidence strength:** **Medium for adjacent search intent; page-specific clicks are not public.**
- **Source checked:** 2026-10-28.

## What this means for Relocation Cost

1. **Do not chase a fabricated “top clicked pages” list.** The only strong public usage figure in this pass is moveBuddha's self-reported 400,000+ annual calculator users. That is not its organic click count and does not establish that every other page gets fewer visits.
2. **Prioritize one excellent utility page over many thin pages.** The strongest examples put the calculator or a direct answer near the top, then explain the estimate and answer related questions.
3. **Keep evidence adjacent to numbers.** Where costs are shown, visitors should see the date/source/methodology and what the number does and does not include.
4. **Earn trust before lead generation.** Anonymous, immediate planning value is a differentiator when some competitors lead toward quote requests.
5. **Use our own Search Console as the real prioritization source.** Claude's 2026-10-28 baseline recorded early impressions for “relocation package calculator” and “calculate relocation costs”; that makes improving the new employer-relocation-package path more defensible than blindly cloning a general moving-cost article. Recheck after a full week of complete data.
6. **Measure the next change:** clicks, impressions, CTR, average position and landing page in our own GSC; on-site calculator starts/completions if analytics are available. Compare the same date windows and account for GSC's reporting delay.
